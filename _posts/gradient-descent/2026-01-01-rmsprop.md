title: RMSProp
order: 6
tex: '\begin{aligned} \mathbf{s}_{k+1} &= \rho\,\mathbf{s}_k + (1-\rho)\,\mathbf{g}_k^2 \\ \mathbf{x}_{k+1} &= \mathbf{x}_k - \eta\,\frac{\mathbf{g}_k}{\sqrt{\mathbf{s}_{k+1}} + \varepsilon} \end{aligned}'
---
Momentum fixed the speed. RMSProp (Hinton, 2012) fixes the direction, with a separate step size for every coordinate. Here $$\mathbf{g}_k = \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)$$, and the square, square root and division are taken entry by entry.

$$\mathbf{s}$$ is a running average of squared gradients, so $$\sqrt{\mathbf{s}}$$ measures how large each coordinate's gradient usually is. Dividing by it turns a gradient of 25 and a gradient of 1 into steps of about the same size. The steep direction is slowed down and the flat one sped up.

Compare with the dashed gradient-descent path: RMSProp heads almost straight at $$\mathbf{x}^\star$$ instead of bouncing between the walls. The curvature mismatch, $$\kappa = 25$$, has been divided away.

This works so well here because the valley is aligned with the axes. A per-coordinate scale cannot undo a valley that runs diagonally. That limitation returns in the Muon block.
