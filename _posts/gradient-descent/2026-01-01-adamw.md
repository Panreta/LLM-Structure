title: AdamW

tex: '\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k - \eta\left(\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_k}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_k} + \varepsilon} + \lambda\,\mathbf{x}_k\right)'
---
Weight decay shrinks the parameters toward $$\mathbf{0}$$ (the dot at the origin) to keep a model from overfitting. With plain gradient descent it is the same as adding $$\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^2$$ to the loss.

With Adam it is not. Put $$\lambda\mathbf{x}$$ into the gradient and Adam divides it by $$\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}}$$ like everything else, so the coordinates with the largest gradients are barely regularized. Loshchilov and Hutter (2017) decoupled it: apply the decay $$\eta\lambda\mathbf{x}$$ directly, outside the adaptive scaling. That is AdamW.

In the figure the path still runs down the valley, but it stops short of $$\mathbf{x}^\star$$ and settles where the decay's pull toward $$\mathbf{0}$$ balances the descent. Raise $$\lambda$$ to pull harder; set it to 0 to get Adam back. The dashed path is Adam.

AdamW, with $$\beta_1 = 0.9$$, $$\beta_2 = 0.95$$ and $$\lambda \approx 0.1$$, is the standard optimizer for training large language models.
