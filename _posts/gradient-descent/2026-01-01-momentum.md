Polyak's heavy ball (1964). Instead of following the current gradient, follow a running sum of past gradients, the velocity $$\mathbf{v}$$.

Along the valley the gradients keep pointing the same way, so they add up and the ball speeds up, up to $$1/(1-\beta)$$ times. Across the valley they alternate in sign and cancel. The dashed path is gradient descent for comparison.

Equivalently, $$\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k - \eta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k) + \beta(\mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}_{k-1})$$: a gradient step plus a fraction of the last move.

On a quadratic the best settings are $$\beta = \left(\frac{\sqrt\kappa - 1}{\sqrt\kappa + 1}\right)^2 = \frac49$$ and $$\eta = \frac{4}{(\sqrt L + \sqrt\mu)^2} = \frac19$$. The error then shrinks by $$\frac{\sqrt\kappa - 1}{\sqrt\kappa + 1} = \frac23$$ per step instead of $$\frac{24}{26}$$: the dependence on $$\kappa$$ becomes a dependence on $$\sqrt\kappa$$.

The price is overshoot. Raise $$\beta$$ toward 0.95 and the ball rolls past the minimum and swings back.
