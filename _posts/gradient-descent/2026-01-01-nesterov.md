---
title: Nesterov momentum
order: 5
tex: '\begin{aligned} \mathbf{v}_{k+1} &= \beta\,\mathbf{v}_k + \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k - \eta\beta\,\mathbf{v}_k) \\ \mathbf{x}_{k+1} & = \mathbf{x}_k - \eta\,\mathbf{v}_{k+1} \end{aligned}'
---
Nesterov's accelerated gradient (1983) changes one thing: it evaluates the gradient where the momentum is about to carry you, $$\mathbf{x}_k - \eta\beta\mathbf{v}_k$$, instead of where you are.

If the ball is about to overshoot, the look-ahead gradient already points back and brakes it early. Compare the blue path with the dashed heavy-ball path at the same $$\eta$$ and $$\beta$$: the swings are damped, and it settles in far fewer steps.

For convex $$f$$ with $$L$$-Lipschitz gradient, a suitably scheduled $$\beta$$ gives

$$f(\mathbf{x}_T) - f^\star = O\!\left(\frac{1}{T^2}\right)$$

versus $$O(1/T)$$ for gradient descent, and no first-order method can do better in general. Deep-learning libraries offer it as the `nesterov=True` flag on SGD.
