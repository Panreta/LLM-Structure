title: 'Solving path: length'
order: 2
tex: 'R_f(q, m)\, R_f(k, n) = R_g(q, k, m - n) \;\Rightarrow\; R_f(\ast, m) = \lVert \ast \rVert'
---
Start in two dimensions and treat $$q, k$$ as complex numbers, $$\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$$; context tells which is meant. The inner product is then

$$\langle q, k \rangle = \mathrm{Re}(q\, k^\ast)$$

so the requirement becomes $$g(q, k, m - n) = \mathrm{Re}\bigl(f(q, m)\, f^\ast(k, n)\bigr)$$. It is enough to ask that $$f(q, m) f^\ast(k, n)$$ itself depends only on $$m - n$$.

Write every complex number by length and angle:

$$f(\ast, m) = R_f(\ast, m)\, e^{\hat i\, \Theta_f(\ast, m)}, \qquad g(q, k, m - n) = R_g(q, k, m - n)\, e^{\hat i\, \Theta_g(q, k, m - n)}$$

Comparing both sides splits the problem in two:

$$R_f(q, m)\, R_f(k, n) = R_g(q, k, m - n), \qquad \Theta_f(q, m) - \Theta_f(k, n) = \Theta_g(q, k, m - n)$$

**Lengths.** Put $$m = n$$ and use the boundary condition:

$$R_f(q, m)\, R_f(k, m) = R_g(q, k, 0) = R_f(q, 0)\, R_f(k, 0) = \lVert q \rVert\, \lVert k \rVert$$

The simplest solution is $$R_f(\ast, m) = \lVert \ast \rVert$$: **the embedding does not change lengths.**

**The figure** shows it. Wherever you drag $$q$$ and $$k$$, and whatever the position $$m$$, the embedded vectors stay on their dashed circles.
