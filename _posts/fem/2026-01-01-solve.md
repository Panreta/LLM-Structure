The load entries are integrals, $$F_i = \int f \phi_i\, dx$$, computed element by element with Gaussian quadrature (three points per element here).

A tridiagonal system is solved directly in $$O(N)$$ operations by Gaussian elimination without pivoting (the Thomas algorithm), which is safe because $$K$$ is symmetric positive definite.

**The figure** compares $$u_h$$ (blue, a broken line through the nodal values) with the exact $$u$$ (black). Even with 4 elements the shape is right; by 16 the two are hard to tell apart.

The readout shows something special to 1-D: the error at the nodes is at rounding level, far smaller than the error between them. For $$-u'' = f$$ the Green's function of each node is itself piecewise linear, so it lies in $$V_h$$, and Galerkin orthogonality then makes $$u_h(x_i) = u(x_i)$$ exactly. Only quadrature limits the nodal accuracy. This **superconvergence** is a 1-D gift; in 2-D the nodal values are good but not exact.
