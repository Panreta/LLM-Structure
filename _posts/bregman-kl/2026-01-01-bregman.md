---
title: Bregman divergence
order: 1
tex: 'B_\varphi(p, q) = \varphi(p) - \varphi(q) - \langle \nabla\varphi(q),\, p - q \rangle'
---
Take a differentiable convex function $$\varphi$$. Its tangent plane at $$q$$ is the first-order Taylor approximation $$\varphi(q) + \langle \nabla\varphi(q), p - q \rangle$$. The **Bregman divergence** is how far $$\varphi(p)$$ lies above that approximation.

**The figure** draws it in one dimension: the blue curve is $$\varphi$$, the dashed line is the tangent at $$q$$, and the brown segment at $$p$$ is $$B_\varphi(p, q)$$. Drag $$p$$ and $$q$$, and switch $$\varphi$$:

- $$\varphi(x) = x^2$$ gives $$B_\varphi(p, q) = (p - q)^2$$: Bregman divergences generalize squared distance.
- $$\varphi(x) = x \log x$$ gives $$p \log\frac pq - p + q$$, one term of a KL divergence (next blocks).
- $$\varphi(x) = e^x$$ gives yet another divergence.

A divergence is not a metric. It is generally not symmetric, $$B_\varphi(p, q) \ne B_\varphi(q, p)$$, and it has no triangle inequality. Move $$p$$ and $$q$$ to opposite sides of each other and compare the gaps.

This is the sense of the GRPO remark that negative entropy and KL relate "like norm and metric": $$\tfrac12\lVert\cdot\rVert^2$$ generates squared distance, and negative entropy generates KL.
