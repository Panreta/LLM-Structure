A physical place where the normal distribution appears exactly: the ground state of the **quantum harmonic oscillator**. A particle of mass $$m$$ sits in a quadratic potential $$V(x) = \tfrac12 m\omega^2 x^2$$. The Schrödinger equation

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' + \tfrac12 m\omega^2 x^2 \psi = E\psi$$

has energies $$E_n = (n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega$$. Its lowest state $$\psi_0$$ is a Gaussian, so the probability of finding the particle at $$x$$ is $$\lvert\psi_0\rvert^2$$, a normal distribution with variance $$\sigma_x^2 = \hbar / (2m\omega)$$.

**The figure** draws the parabola, the first four levels with their wavefunctions, and $$\lvert\psi_0\rvert^2$$ in blue. Raise $$\omega$$ (a stiffer trap) and the Gaussian narrows.

**Minimum uncertainty.** The momentum distribution of $$\psi_0$$ is also normal, with $$\sigma_p^2 = m\hbar\omega / 2$$, so $$\sigma_x \sigma_p = \hbar/2$$. That is the smallest product the Heisenberg uncertainty principle allows, and Gaussians are the only states that reach it.

**In three dimensions.** For an isotropic trap $$V = \tfrac12 m\omega^2 \lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^2$$, the ground state factorizes, $$\psi_0(\mathbf{x}) = \psi_0(x)\psi_0(y)\psi_0(z) \propto e^{-m\omega\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^2/2\hbar}$$. Its probability cloud is exactly the 3-D normal ball of block 3, with $$\sigma^2 = \hbar/(2m\omega)$$: dense at the center, thinning outward, and with zero-point energy $$\tfrac32\hbar\omega$$.

**Where you meet it.**

- A single atom or ion cooled to the ground state of a laser or ion trap: its position spread is this Gaussian, tens of nanometres across.
- The vibrations of a diatomic molecule such as H$$_2$$ near its equilibrium bond length, where the potential is nearly quadratic.
- A Bose–Einstein condensate of non-interacting atoms in a harmonic trap, where every atom occupies this same Gaussian cloud.
