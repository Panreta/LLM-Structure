The brown arrows. On its eigenlines $$A$$ only stretches: $$A\mathbf{v}_1 = 3\mathbf{v}_1$$ and $$A\mathbf{v}_2 = \mathbf{v}_2$$, so each component is multiplied by its own eigenvalue.

The slider applies $$A_t = (1-t)I + tA$$. Every $$A_t$$ has the same eigenvectors, with eigenvalues $$1 + 2t$$ and $$1$$. Move $$t$$ and watch: the grid shears, but the brown arrows never leave their lines.

This is why powers are easy: $$A^k = P\,D^k\,P^{-1}$$.
