---
title: Geometric Brownian motion
order: 6
tex: 'dS_t = \mu S_t\, dt + \sigma S_t\, dW_t \;\Longrightarrow\; S_t = S_0 \exp\!\Bigl(\bigl(\mu - \tfrac12\sigma^2\bigr)t + \sigma W_t\Bigr)'
---
**Model.** A stock price $$S_t > 0$$ whose returns have constant mean rate $$\mu$$ and volatility $$\sigma > 0$$:

$$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = \mu\, dt + \sigma\, dW_t$$

**Solution.** Apply Itô's lemma to $$f(x) = \ln x$$, with $$f' = 1/x$$ and $$f'' = -1/x^2$$:

$$d\ln S_t = \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - \frac{1}{2}\frac{(dS_t)^2}{S_t^2} = \Bigl(\mu - \tfrac12\sigma^2\Bigr)dt + \sigma\, dW_t$$

Integrate and exponentiate to get the formula above. The coefficients are Lipschitz, so this is the unique strong solution. Since it is an exponential, $$S_t > 0$$ always.

**Distribution.** $$\ln S_t \sim \mathcal{N}\bigl(\ln S_0 + (\mu - \tfrac12\sigma^2)t,\ \sigma^2 t\bigr)$$, so $$S_t$$ is **lognormal**. Using $$\mathbb{E}e^{\sigma W_t} = e^{\sigma^2 t/2}$$:

$$\mathbb{E}S_t = S_0 e^{\mu t}, \qquad \operatorname{median}(S_t) = S_0 e^{(\mu - \sigma^2/2)t}, \qquad \operatorname{Var} S_t = S_0^2 e^{2\mu t}\bigl(e^{\sigma^2 t} - 1\bigr)$$

The median grows more slowly than the mean, at rate $$\mu - \tfrac12\sigma^2$$. With large volatility the typical path can even fall while the average rises: try $$\mu = 0.1$$, $$\sigma = 0.6$$. This is the Itô correction again, now in the drift of $$\ln S$$.
