---
title: Mass on the shell
order: 3
tex: '\frac{V_n (1-\varepsilon)^n}{V_n} = (1 - \varepsilon)^n \to 0'
---
How much of the ball lies deep inside? The inner ball of radius $$1 - \varepsilon$$ holds a fraction $$(1 - \varepsilon)^n$$ of the volume, which goes to 0 however small $$\varepsilon$$ is. In high dimensions almost all of the volume sits in a thin shell at the surface.

The figure shows the same fact as a density. The radius $$r$$ of a uniform random point in $$B^n$$ has distribution $$P(R \le r) = r^n$$, so its density is $$n r^{n-1}$$. The brown area is the share within $$\varepsilon$$ of the surface.

At $$n = 20$$ and $$\varepsilon = 0.1$$ that share is already 88%; at $$n = 200$$ it is all but $$10^{-9}$$. A rule of thumb: the shell that holds most of the mass has thickness about $$1/n$$.
