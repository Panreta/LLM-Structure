TRPO works, but it is heavy: conjugate gradient, Hessian-vector products and a line search at every update. It also fits poorly with large networks that share parameters between policy and value heads.

**Proximal Policy Optimization** (Schulman et al., 2017) goes back to the same trust-region problem and drops the linear approximation. Instead of solving the constraint exactly, it builds it into the objective, then runs plain stochastic gradient ascent (Adam) for a few epochs on minibatches of the collected samples.

**The figure** shows what is at stake. The blue dot is the exact solution of the trust-region problem in the running example. The brown ring is where TRPO's linear-quadratic step would land: with a large $$\delta$$ its true KL can be far from the target. There are two ways to keep the update proximal without that machinery, the next two chapters:

- **PPO-Penalty:** subtract $$\beta \cdot \text{KL}$$ from the objective.
- **PPO-Clip:** clip the probability ratio so that moving too far stops paying.
