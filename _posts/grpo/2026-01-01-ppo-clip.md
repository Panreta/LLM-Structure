---
title: PPO-Clip
order: 10
tex: '\arg\max_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{s,\, a \sim \pi_{\theta_k}}\Bigl[\min\Bigl\{ r_\theta A^{\pi_{\theta_k}}(s, a),\ \text{clip}(r_\theta;\, 1-\varepsilon, 1+\varepsilon)\, A^{\pi_{\theta_k}}(s, a) \Bigr\}\Bigr]'
---
Here $$r_\theta = \pi_\theta(a \mid s) / \pi_{\theta_k}(a \mid s)$$ and $$\text{clip}(x; l, u) = \max\{\min\{x, u\}, l\}$$ restricts $$x$$ to $$[l, u]$$.

**The figure** is the objective for one sample as a function of the ratio. When the advantage is positive, raising the action's probability pays off only until $$r = 1 + \varepsilon$$; after that the objective is flat and its gradient is 0. When the advantage is negative, the same happens below $$1 - \varepsilon$$. The $$\min$$ makes it a pessimistic bound: clipping can remove an incentive, but never add one. A ratio that has moved the wrong way still gets the full unclipped gradient.

**Question 3.** Can clipping fully replace the penalty? In practice mostly yes. Clip enforces a hard per-sample limit on how far each ratio can usefully move, while the penalty only discourages a large *average* KL, a looser region. So clipping is usually more stable numerically and has become the default, while the penalty tolerates more. Neither guarantees the KL constraint exactly.

**Back to GRPO.** The introduction's formula is now PPO-Clip with three changes:

1. **No value network.** The advantage $$\hat A_{i,t}$$ is the reward normalized within a group of $$G$$ answers to the same question. For a language model this removes a critic as large as the policy itself.
2. **Per-token averaging.** Each answer's tokens are averaged with weight $$1/\lvert o_i \rvert$$, then the answers are averaged over the group.
3. **KL to a reference model** $$\pi_{\text{ref}}$$, usually the starting model, is subtracted directly in the loss instead of being folded into the reward. It is estimated per token with $$\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}}{\pi_\theta} - \log\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}}{\pi_\theta} - 1$$, which is always non-negative.

Not so terrifying after all.
