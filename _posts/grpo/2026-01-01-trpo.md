**Trust Region Policy Optimization** (Schulman et al., 2015) solves the trust-region problem approximately. Expand both sides around $$\theta$$: linearly for the objective, quadratically for the constraint (the KL and its gradient vanish at $$\theta' = \theta$$):

$$L_\theta(\theta') - J(\theta) \approx g^\top(\theta' - \theta), \qquad \mathbb{E}_s\bigl[D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta \,\Vert\, \pi_{\theta'})\bigr] \approx \tfrac12 (\theta' - \theta)^\top H (\theta' - \theta)$$

with $$g = \nabla_{\theta'} L_\theta(\theta')$$ and $$H$$ the Hessian of the average KL, both at $$\theta' = \theta$$. The problem becomes

$$\max_{\theta'}\ g^\top(\theta' - \theta) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \tfrac12(\theta' - \theta)^\top H (\theta' - \theta) \le \delta$$

**Question 2.** Is $$H$$ positive definite? The KL is $$\ge 0$$ and equals 0 at $$\theta' = \theta$$, so that point is a minimum and $$H$$ is positive semidefinite. In fact $$H$$ is the **Fisher information matrix** $$\mathbb{E}[\nabla \log \pi_\theta\, \nabla \log \pi_\theta^\top]$$. It is positive definite unless some direction of $$\theta$$ leaves the policy unchanged.

**Remark 3.** This is maximizing a linear function over an ellipse, which always has a solution, on the boundary. That is the figure: gray lines are level sets of $$g^\top x$$, the gray region is the ellipse $$\tfrac12 x^\top H x \le \delta$$, and the KKT conditions put the optimum where a level line touches the ellipse. The answer is the formula above. Note that the best direction is $$H^{-1}g$$ (blue), the **natural gradient**, not $$g$$ itself (gray). Drag $$g$$ to see the gap change.
